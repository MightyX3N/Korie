What it does

What works today, and what's being built. Nothing below is marked as working unless it was checked with the real apps.

Works

Ready when you add a folder

Films and series are listed from their file and folder names straight away, in rows by category. Posters and descriptions from TMDb follow in the background, and a file is only looked into when someone presses play.

Works

Korie's own TV app

Home, Films, Series, Music, Live TV, Search and Settings, made for the remote. It can be the TV's home screen, with your apps on Home, and it updates itself from the releases here.

Works

Who's watching?

Everyone in the house is a person in Korie, with a PIN if they like and an age limit for children. The limit is applied on the server, so every app gets the same list.

Works

Add-ons in Films, Series and Live TV

DRTV (with DR profiles per person), Stremio (add-ons for everyone, or your own Stremio account with Continue Watching both ways), Free Films and RomM. Add-ons are Lua and install from the Store without a restart.

Works

Downloads into the library

Download a film, an episode, a season or a whole series from DRTV or Stremio, with subtitles, or follow a series and get new episodes as they come out.

Works

Plays what the device can

A file the TV plays itself is sent as it is. Anything else is converted with FFmpeg while it plays, subtitles included. Live TV comes through the server too.

Works

TV channels from your library

Channels in Live TV that play your own films and series around the clock: one per genre, plus Classics and Kids, with a guide for the week. Something above a child's age limit is covered until a grown-up types their PIN.

Works

Admin in the browser

Libraries, people, add-ons with their accounts and settings, downloads and the server's log, at /web/admin.

Works

Jellyfin's apps too

Jellyfin's apps find Korie on the home network and play from it. Checked with the Android TV app and the web app.

Being built

Calendar, outside the home, households

A calendar of new episodes, watching from outside the home with playit.gg, and households with their own sign-in.

Korie or Jellyfin?

An honest comparison. Korie is new and small, and Jellyfin has years of work behind it. Where Jellyfin is better, it says so.

Pick Korie if

  • You want a library that's ready the moment you add it
  • You want TV sources like DR TV or Stremio inside Films and Series
  • Your house watches films and series on Android TV and in the browser

Stay with Jellyfin if

  • You need recordings, or apps on phones and other devices today
  • You rely on hardware transcoding for many viewers at once
  • You use features Korie hasn't been checked with yet

Get started

  1. On the NAS, paste this into the Docker app as a project. Change /volume1/Media to your media folder and /volume1/docker/korie to where Korie keeps its data.

  2. Open http://<nas>:8100, then Admin: add your folders under Libraries, the people in the house under People, and add-ons under Add-ons.

  3. On the TV, install Korie's TV app (with an app like Downloader, or from a USB stick). It finds Korie by itself. After that it updates itself. Jellyfin's Android TV app works too: pick Korie under Discovered servers.

services:
  korie:
    image: ghcr.io/mightyx3n/korie:latest
    container_name: korie
    restart: unless-stopped
    network_mode: host        # lets the TVs find Korie by themselves
    environment:
      - TZ=Europe/Copenhagen
    volumes:
      - /volume1/docker/korie:/data
      - /volume1/Media:/media    # without :ro, so downloads can be saved into it

Korie is open source: github.com/MightyX3N/Korie.

Korie talks to Jellyfin's apps through Jellyfin's published API and contains no Jellyfin code. Korie is not made by or connected to the Jellyfin project. Film and series details come from TMDb; Korie uses the TMDb API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDb.
